Trade Marks Act, 1999 — Protection of Ayurvedic Product Names and Brands

1. Registrability of Ayurvedic Product Names:
Under the Trade Marks Act 1999, you can register a trademark for an Ayurvedic product name, brand, or logo. The mark must be distinctive and capable of distinguishing goods or services of one enterprise from those of others.

2. Grounds for Refusal (Section 9):
- Generic or Descriptive Names: Generic Ayurvedic terms and botanical names (e.g., "Ashwagandha", "Triphala", "Churna", "Rasayana") cannot be trademarked for products containing those ingredients because they are descriptive of the goods or common to trade.
- Distinctive & Coined Names: Coined or fanciful Sanskrit and herbal names that are distinctive and not directly descriptive of the product's composition, quality, or intended purpose can be registered.
- Public Domain & Deceptive Marks: Marks that deceive the public about geographic origin or nature of the Ayurvedic medicine are barred.

3. International Registration:
Indian brand owners can protect Ayurvedic trademarks internationally across multiple jurisdictions using the Madrid System (Madrid Protocol) administered by WIPO, filing through the Indian Trademark Registry.
